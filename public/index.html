Our place

Named after jasmine.

Yiasemi (Γιασεμί) is Greek for jasmine, the small white flower that scents the courtyards of old Athens on summer nights.

Cushions on the marble steps, small tables, and an easy pace from morning until late. Sit, order a coffee, and watch Plaka go by.

A small guide

How to order Greek coffee.

The sugar goes into the briki while it brews, so you choose before it's made.

Σκέτος[ské-tos]

No sugar. Strong and bitter, the purist's cup.

Μέτριος[mé-tri-os]

Medium. One teaspoon of sugar, the most popular order.

Γλυκός[gli-kós]

Sweet. Two teaspoons, rich and smooth.

Tip: let the grounds settle for a minute, sip slowly, and leave the last mouthful in the cup.

Good to know

Questions guests ask.

Where is Yiasemi café?

Yiasemi is at Mnisikleous 23 in Plaka, Athens (105 55), on the stepped pedestrian street that climbs toward the Acropolis.

What is the nearest metro station?

Monastiraki (lines 1 and 3) is about a 7-minute walk away. Syntagma and Akropoli stations are around 15 minutes on foot.

What should I order at Yiasemi?

Try a traditional Greek coffee, a homemade spinach or cheese pie, and orange cake (portokalopita) with mastic ice cream. In the evening, a warm rakomelo.

Can I sit outside?

Yes. Yiasemi is known for its seating with cushions on the marble steps of Mnisikleous Street.

Is it close to the Acropolis?

Yes. Mnisikleous Street leads up toward the Acropolis; the entrance is roughly a 12-minute uphill walk.

What are the opening hours?

Hours can change with the season, so please call +30 213 041 7937 before you come.
